# Figures

Generated by `python run_experiments.py`. Regenerate at full scale with
`--full` on a GPU.

> **These are demo-scale.** Produced with a few hundred training rows and a
> handful of epochs on CPU. They demonstrate that the machinery runs and
> that the qualitative claims hold; they are **not** publishable numbers.
> Accuracy figures in particular will be poor — that is expected at this
> subset size and is not evidence about the method.
>
> Two figures are exceptions and are exact at any scale, because they are
> analytic rather than trained: `e5_temperature.png` and `e0_leakage.png`.

---

### `e0_leakage.png` — split integrity
How much of each test set the model has already seen during training.

On Davis the random split puts **100% of test drugs and 100% of test
targets** in the training set, because the affinity matrix is dense and
there are only 68 distinct compounds. Cold and scaffold regimes are the
only meaningful ones for this dataset. Report this table alongside any
accuracy claim.

### `e1_splits.png` — accuracy by split regime
Test MSE and concordance index across random / cold-drug / cold-target /
scaffold.

The gap between random and cold is the quantity worth reporting at full
scale. A method that looks strong only on the random split has not been
shown to generalise to new compounds or new targets.

**Read this one with care.** At demo scale the differences between regimes
are not meaningful — in the shipped run cold-target scores *better* than
random, which is noise, not a finding. The expected ordering appears only
with enough training data and epochs for the model to actually exploit the
leakage that `e0_leakage.png` documents. Do not quote these numbers.

Note also that on Davis, `cold_drug` and `scaffold` are the same split:
all 68 compounds have distinct Bemis–Murcko scaffolds. Use KIBA for a
meaningful scaffold evaluation.

### `e2_frontier.png` — the faithfulness–accuracy frontier
Three panels: accuracy against budget, faithfulness against budget, and
the two plotted against each other with the dense softmax model marked.

This is the figure the research plan is built around: **what does
verifiable explanation cost?** A small accuracy penalty for a large
faithfulness gain means dense models are spending capacity on something
other than interaction modelling. A large penalty quantifies the price of
honesty. Both outcomes are results.

### `e3_deletion_curves.png` — does the explanation move the prediction?
Delete the contacts an explanation ranks highest and watch the output
move; compare against deleting at random, and against deleting the
*least* important.

A faithful explanation gives **most > random > least**. The headline
scalar is **AFS** (above-random faithfulness), the normalised gap between
the "most" and "random" curves. AFS ≤ 0 means the explanation is no better
than chance.

The x-axis is log-scaled. Linear spacing hides the whole story for a
sparse model, whose entire support is deleted within the first few
percent.

### `e3_exactness.png` — attribution vs causal effect
Whether |Δprediction| equals the attributed importance of the deleted
contacts.

For the additive bottleneck this holds to machine precision (residual
~1e-7) because it is true by construction. For the pooled DrugBAN-style
control it fails by several pKd units. That residual is a direct measure
of **how much prediction mass bypasses the interaction map** — the
quantity that makes "interpretable attention" in pooled models
questionable.

Note that the pooled control can still score a respectable AFS: its
attention magnitudes do correlate with importance. What it cannot do is
tell you *how much* a contact is worth. Correlation without calibration.

### `e4_support_dynamics.png` — the collapse watch
Training loss and mean support size over epochs, on twin axes.

**Support is the number to watch during any real training run.** If it
collapses toward 1, the model has degenerated into an argmax that keeps a
single contact. It will still train, the loss will still fall, and the
sparsity will look impressive — while the explanations are empty. This
exact failure happened during development (an inverted bisection in
α-entmax) and was invisible to every test that only checked the decoder.

### `e5_temperature.png` — sparsity calibration
Support size as a function of the learned temperature, for several
candidate-pair counts. Analytic, so exact at any scale.

After per-sample logit standardisation, support depends on temperature and
on the number of candidate pairs, but **not** on embedding width. Before
standardisation it did depend on embedding width (logit std ≈ 15 at
`H_DIM=16`, ≈ 133 at `H_DIM=128`), which would have silently confounded
every budget sweep.

### `e6_ablations.png` — what each component contributes
Accuracy, faithfulness and support size for four variants: entmax with
adaptive budget, entmax with fixed budget, hard top-k, and dense softmax.

The dense softmax row is the important control. It keeps the additive
decoder but removes sparsity, which isolates how much of the behaviour
comes from the bottleneck rather than from the decode.
